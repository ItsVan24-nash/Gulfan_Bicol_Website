Our History & Culture

Stories carved by volcanoes, faith, and resilient people.

Our Story

A Land Shaped by Fire

The Bikolano People

The region takes its name from the Bikol people and the Bicol River, which flows through Camarines Sur. Long before foreign ships arrived, communities here fished, farmed abaca and rice, and traded across the sea.

Spanish Era and Faith

Spanish missionaries built churches and towns across the peninsula, and Catholic devotion became part of everyday life. Camarines was long administered as a single province, then divided into Camarines Norte and Camarines Sur in 1919.

Mayon and Resilience

Mayon Volcano has erupted many times, including the 1814 eruption that buried the town of Cagsawa. Bikolanos continue to live in its shadow with respect, and the volcano remains both a danger and a symbol of pride.

Cultural Highlights

Festivals, Crafts, and Food

Festivals

  • Magayon Festival in Albay celebrates the legend of Daragang Magayon.
  • Peñafrancia Festival in Naga City honors Our Lady of Peñafrancia every September.
  • Pili Festival in Sorsogon celebrates the pili tree and its nut.
  • Rodeo Masbateño in Masbate City showcases the island’s cowboy culture.
  • Abaca Festival in Catanduanes highlights the fiber that supports many families.

Crafts & Foods

  • Abaca weaving turns local fiber into bags, mats, and fashion pieces.
  • Pili nut sweets are a famous pasalubong from the region.
  • Bicol Express is a spicy stew of pork, chili, and coconut milk.
  • Laing is taro leaves simmered in coconut milk.
  • Kinalas is a hearty noodle soup from Naga City.
